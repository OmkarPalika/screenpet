A woof, a meow, a chirp — whichever this one is — when it says something. Made up on the spot rather than played from a file.

Roams the whole screen instead of pacing the bottom of it, and now and then climbs up to sit on the top edge of whichever window you just switched to. It is handed a rectangle and nothing else — not the title, not which application — so it has no idea what it just sat on, and it cannot touch your windows: it moves itself, and that is the whole of it.

Every so often the pet thinks about a glass of water, or about sitting still — a little thought bubble beside it, alternating between the two. Nothing happens unless you click it: ignored, it goes away on its own. Click it and the pet dims the screen, walks into the middle and takes the break itself while you watch. Click anywhere to stop it early. It never appears while Windows is set to do not disturb — a game, a call, a presentation — and time away from the machine counts as the break.

For example CommandOrControl+Shift+Space.

Reads the window you are actually working in rather than everything on the screen. Falls back to the whole screen by itself when that would not help — a maximised window, or one mostly on the other monitor.

Normally the pet reads your screen when you press the hotkey and at no other time. With this on it reads it on the timer below as well, and answers when it finds a question — otherwise it says nothing at all, which is most of the time. It skips a screen it has already read, stays quiet while Windows is set to do not disturb, and stops entirely while you are away from the machine. An amber dot on the pet is lit for as long as it is on. Only with a model on this machine — choosing a hosted provider switches this off, because reading your screen every minute and sending each read to a company is not the same decision as doing it when you press a key.

Adds Listen… to the pet's menu. The speech is recognised on this machine; no audio is saved or sent anywhere.

Both run on this machine and neither reaches the network. Windows' own recogniser needs no setup and is what the pet has always used — measured on real speech it got 88% of words wrong, and scored every result below its own confidence floor, which is why it so often says it did not catch that. On the same recordings whisper.cpp scored 21% and Parakeet 19%. Neither is bundled: put whisper-cli.exe or parakeet-cli.exe — plus model.bin — in the app's whisper folder and this picks up whichever is there. Parakeet is better at commands and says nothing when you said nothing; whisper is a third the size and better at code words.

This holds the microphone open for as long as it is on — that is what a wake word costs, and there is no version of it that does not. Windows does the listening, on this machine, with a grammar containing only the wake phrases: it is not a transcriber looking for a word, it is incapable of recognising anything else. The only thing that ever comes back from it is the fact that you said it.

Turns Listen… into a conversation: it answers, then listens again, without a click each time. It does not hold the microphone open — it reopens it for each turn and closes it in between, and the first turn where you say nothing ends it. So do ten turns, three minutes, and anything else you do with the pet.

Holds the microphone open. The pet listens for a beat and moves on it. The audio is turned into one number — how loud, right now — and thrown away; nothing is recognised, recorded or sent. Off, it still dances when you ask, and opens the microphone only for the length of the dance.

Uses the camera to tell whether anything moved, and nothing else. Frames are shrunk to 32×24, compared with the one before, and discarded — it cannot recognise a face, and nothing is saved or sent. A green dot on the pet shows whenever the camera is open. Ask it to take a photo and it keeps that one frame, in your Pictures folder — the only frame it ever writes down, and still nothing it sends anywhere.

Uses the face detector built into the operating system, so the pet can tell the difference between a person sitting down and a door opening. It answers how many faces and nothing else — no name, no identity, no template kept. The frame is handed to Windows in memory and never written to disk.

Off by default, and off is the point of this app. While it is off the pet is sealed in: a model on this machine, and anything that would need the network says so instead of doing it. Turning it on sends nothing by itself — it unlocks the settings below, each of which is its own decision. Turning it back off switches them off with it.

Asking about the weather sends the town below to open-meteo.com — no account, no key, nothing from your screen, camera or microphone, and no location lookup. Off, the pet says it cannot see outside, which is the truth.

look up the speed of light sends those words to DuckDuckGo and Wikipedia — no key, no account, so nothing ties the lookup to you. Only what you typed after the command goes; nothing from your screen, memory, camera or microphone.

Stored in keys.json, wrapped with Windows DPAPI under your account — not in the settings file, and never shown again once stored. Copying that file to another machine or another user gets an unreadable lump. It is not a vault: anything running as you can unwrap it, exactly as the pet does.

Version

Nothing is checked on a timer and nothing downloads until you press the second button. The only thing that leaves this machine is a request for the release list — no settings, no screen, no memory, no account and no identifier go with it. The installer that comes back is checked against the release manifest before it is run, and it installs over the copy you already have rather than opening the setup wizard again.

Say remember my standup is at 9 and it writes that down, on this machine, and brings it up later. Only what you tell it to remember is written — never your chat, your screen, the microphone or the camera. Everything else it picks up is a counter: which hour you tend to ask it things, how often you looked after it today. Say what do you remember to read it back, forget the standup to drop one, or forget everything to empty it. Switching this off deletes the file.

The pet occasionally brings up what it remembers to needle you — how many headpats you managed on your best day against today, the hour you always turn up, the thing you told it you would do. Every one of those numbers is real; it teases with what it has, not with anything invented. At most one remark every ninety minutes, and never during quiet hours.
